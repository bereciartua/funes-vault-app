# ADR-0028: Search answers: snippets that show the matching sentence, and an Ask that quotes your memories, on the Mac and the iPhone

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-29 (corrected the same day after a GPT-6-Astra review of the spec: matches from FTS5, keyword-only passages, sources in the prompt, no disconnect cancellation, no highlight offsets) · **Amends** [FR-V-10](../../product/03-requirements.md) (search), [FR-AI-05](../../product/03-requirements.md) (tasks), [FR-AI-08](../../product/03-requirements.md) (disclosure), [FR-AI-15](../../product/03-requirements.md) (daily cap), [FR-iOS-07](../../product/03-requirements.md) (iPhone search), [model providers §1, §3–6 and §9](../model-providers.md), [memory processing](../../domain/memory-processing.md) (new §10), [companion protocol §4 and §7](../companion-protocol.md), [security and privacy §3 and §5](../security-and-privacy.md), [UX: Mac ▸ Vault and Settings](../../product/04-ux-mac.md), [UX: iPhone ▸ Search and Settings](../../product/05-ux-iphone.md); adds FR-V-17, FR-AI-17 and FR-iOS-13

**Context.**
- **Search finds the right memories, but the fact is buried.** The author's feedback after daily use of both apps: for "What's my title at Chatham?" the right memories come back on the first page, but the answer sits in the middle of one result's body, and an older memory says something else. Today a result's snippet is the first 160 characters of its body (`CompanionHandlers.snippet`, and the Vault's one-line preview), so the sentence that answers is usually cut off, and the only way to the fact is opening memory after memory.
- **The author asked for an optional, configurable model step** that reads the question and the first page of results and narrows them down or answers, on the Mac and the iPhone, keeping search as it is.
- **Four designs were compared** with mockups (the proposal "Funes Search Answers", revision 2): **A** an answer card that appears on its own for question-shaped searches; **B** an Ask button that answers on demand; **C** pinpointing, where the model only picks the answering memories and sentences and the list reorders; **D** a conversation with follow-ups. GPT-6-Astra reviewed the proposal against this repository; its findings and the responses are recorded in the proposal.
- **Speed decides the trigger.** [Spike E](../../plans/spike-results.md#1-apple-on-device) measured, on the author's 8 GB M1: Apple on-device about 32 s a call (and its quotes often empty or carrying prompt tags), Claude Haiku through Claude Code about 5 s, GPT-5.6-Terra through Codex about 7 s, GPT-6-Luna 21 s, Ollama qwen3 4B 131 s. Those are extraction calls, not answers, but no available model is fast enough for an answer that appears on its own (about 3 s) without an API key.
- **The companion channel gives a call 10 s** ([§7](../companion-protocol.md#7-limits-per-device)), and the phone waits 15 s. A model call doesn't fit.
- **The iPhone has no model** ([model providers §1](../model-providers.md#1-tasks-that-use-a-model)): everything is computed on the Mac.

**Decision.**
1. **Snippets show the sentence that matches the search** (FR-V-17). The Vault list, and `memories.search`'s `snippet` for the iPhone, show the passage of the body that best matches the search's terms, trimmed around the match, instead of the body's first 160 characters. No model and no new term rules: FTS5's `highlight()` with the search's own MATCH query says where the body matches, so snippets agree with keyword search, and FR-V-10's 150 ms holds. The match positions go with the snippet (`snippetMatches`), and both apps highlight them. The snippet field and its length don't change, so an older iPhone app just gets better snippets.
2. **Search answers are a new model task, off by default** (FR-AI-17). Settings ▸ AI Models gets a **Search answers** row: **Answer** (Off · Only when I ask) and **Model** (Same as New memories, or any configured generation provider). There is no automatic mode in v0.6.
3. **The answer is asked for.** In the Vault (⌘⏎ in the search field, or the **Ask Funes** row on top of the results) and in the iPhone's Search (an **Ask Funes** pill), on a non-empty search in the Active scope that has results.
4. **It appears in place, and the results don't move.** The Ask row becomes a card at the top of the list: "Reading 8 results…" with **Cancel**, then the answer. Results are never reordered, filtered or selected for the user. Changing the search, scope or filters clears the card.
5. **Every claim quotes a memory.** The model returns at most 3 claims, each citing one or more of the memories it was given with a verbatim quote. The code checks each quote against the exact text that was sent for that memory; a claim with any quote that fails is dropped, and the card says so. With no claim left, the card says "No answer in the 8 results read." Each claim shows its quote, and its number opens the memory with the quoted sentence highlighted.
6. **What the model reads.** The search text, today's date, and the top 8 active, unexpired results of the search as shown (5 for Apple on-device, whose window is about 4,000 tokens): title, kind, categories, created and updated dates, its source (you, an agent, an import or shared text: the prompt never claims the user wrote everything), and the body, whole up to 1,500 characters (600 for Apple on-device, trimmed further to fit its window), otherwise its best-matching passages by keyword match, with a sentence either side. Conflicting memories are shown as such ("An older memory says…"); the code doesn't decide which date wins.
7. **Same guardrails as every other task.** One call through `ModelRouter.send`. For a cloud provider, memories above the cloud limit are left out before the prompt is built, and the card says how many ("1 memory above Internal wasn't read"). The search text is an explicit query item, as it already is for provider embeddings: the secret scanner applies, the cloud limit applies to the memories, and memories of unknown sensitivity still fail closed. If every memory is withheld, the Mac answers "no answer" itself and sends nothing. A cloud call that passes the guard counts once against the shared daily cap (not in the agents' share), even if it then fails or is cancelled. Subscription CLIs run isolated as in ADR-0024.
8. **Nothing is kept.** No answer cache: an answer lives on screen until the search changes. Activity logs `search_answered` with counts, the engine and the duration, never text, as the user or as `device:<id>` via the companion.
9. **No fallback to another model.** If the chosen model fails, times out (45 s) or is unavailable, the card says why with **Try Again**. Falling back to Apple on-device would mean a 30 s wait for weaker quotes.
10. **The iPhone asks the Mac.** A new companion method, `memories.answer {query, filters?, ids}`, with the ids of the results the phone is showing, never memory text. The Mac re-reads them as the owner, runs the same pipeline and returns the claims with ids and quotes; the app that opens a memory finds the quote in it to highlight it, so no offsets travel. It has its own deadline (50 s on the Mac, 55 s on the phone), is never resent automatically, a new call from the same device cancels the previous one, and returns typed errors that the phone never shows as "Mac not reachable". `status.get` says whether answers are on and which engine answers, so the phone shows Ask only when the Mac will answer. The phone can also hide Ask for itself.
11. **Quality is measured before the answer step ships.** Spike G (Phase 9.2) runs about 40 questions on the benchmark vault, plus a held-out set written blind by another model, with Claude Haiku and GPT-5.6-Terra (and one Apple on-device run for the record). Gates: correct claims ≥ 95%, the right "no answer" ≥ 90%, answered ≥ 80% of answerable questions, p95 ≤ 15 s. The results may change this ADR's numbers (top 8, 1,500 characters, 45 s), recorded in the same change. *Result ([Spike G](../../plans/spike-results.md#spike-g-search-answer-quality), 29 Oct 2026): the three cloud providers passed on 62 questions (Luna the most balanced: 98% correct claims, every abstention and conflict, p95 5.0 s; Terra made no wrong claim but often dropped the older side of a conflict); Apple on-device failed. The numbers stay; the prompt gained one rule (a claim that only says what isn't recorded isn't an answer).*

**Consequences.**
- **Two features, shipped in order.** Snippets (Phase 9.1) ship first and may make Ask unnecessary for many searches; the author uses them before the answer step is built.
- **New text leaves the Mac, only when asked.** The search text and the top results' text (up to the cloud limit) go to the Search answers provider. It's a new row in [security and privacy §5](../security-and-privacy.md#5-everything-that-can-leave-the-mac) and in PRIVACY.md. With the default (Off) nothing changes.
- **A wrong answer is possible.** Quotes prove where a claim came from, not that it's right: a claim can misread a correct quote. Mitigations: claims are short, each shows its quote, the results stay visible under the card, and the spike gates correctness and abstention. Recorded as T37.
- **The companion gets its first slow method.** `CompanionClient`'s single call timeout becomes per method. Cancel on the phone only stops waiting: the listener can't see a closed connection while a handler runs (`CompanionListener` pauses reading), and reworking it isn't worth it for one user. The Mac's work ends at its result, the deadline, or the same device's next Ask.
- **The daily cap is shared by one more task.** Each Ask is one cloud request (a retry is another); at the cap the card says so and search keeps working.
- **Tests:** snippets (the FTS5 match spans, sentences, trimming, `snippetMatches`, the 150 ms budget); the answer's validation (quotes outside the sent text, unknown refs, empty or too-short quotes, too many claims); guardrails (nothing above the limit and no secret in a cloud prompt; the search text through the scanner); the cap (counted after the guard only); the on-device window; cancellation on the Mac ending the CLI process; `memories.answer`'s contract and errors; the iPhone's states, including demo mode.

**Alternatives considered.**
- **A, an answer that appears on its own for questions:** zero steps when it's fast, but nothing available is under 3 s on the author's Mac, a question heuristic misses "Chatham title" and Spanish, and it spends the shared daily cap on every question-shaped search. Kept as a later option (Phase 9.7), behind a measured gate: p50 ≤ 3 s and p95 ≤ 5 s on the author's Mac.
- **B's popover or sheet:** hides the results the answer cites. The card in place keeps them visible.
- **C, pinpointing with reordering:** shows only the user's own words, but can't combine two memories, and moving rows under the cursor is disorienting. Its strength survives as the quote under every claim, and snippets (decision 1) get much of its benefit without a model.
- **D, a conversation with follow-ups:** the most capable and the most work (thread UI on two platforms, retrieval per turn, context that Apple on-device can't hold), and it overlaps with what agents already do over MCP. Deferred.
- **Apple on-device as the default:** sends nothing off the Mac, but Spike E's 32 s a call and unreliable quotes make it a poor first experience. It stays a choice, with a status line saying it's slow.
- **Falling back to on-device when a cloud model fails or memories are above the limit:** a 30 s wait for weaker quotes. The card says what happened instead.
- **A short-lived answer cache:** an answer would need keys for memory versions, filters, the provider and the cloud limit to stay correct. With Ask on demand there's little to gain; no cache.
